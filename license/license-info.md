## **Permission for Use and Modification**
Under copyright law, we own the exclusive rights to our code and data. Without a license, others cannot legally copy, modify, or distribute our work, even if it is primarily hosted on a public GitHub repository.

The Benefit: It explicitly grants permission for others to build upon our housing affordability research and reuse our automated data pipeline.

## **Liability Protection** 
The capitalized section at the bottom of our license serves as a crucial legal shield.

The Benefit: It states that the software is provided "AS IS". If someone uses our pipeline to make a financial decision or a business investment and the data contains an error, this clause protects us from being held legally responsible (liable) for those damages.

## **Promoting Reproducibility**
A core goal of our IS477 course backing this project is "Data Management, Curation & Reproducibility." The third element is crucial to the final segment of the course.

The Benefit: A license is a key metadata element. It tells future researchers exactly what they are allowed to do with our integrated Zillow and Census dataset. Without it, our project isn't truly "reproducible" because others wouldn't have the legal right to run our scripts. This is a contradiction that is impossible to solve without legal covenants like licenses.

## **Simple Attribution**
The MIT license requires that anyone who uses a significant portion of our software must include our original copyright notice.

The Benefit: This license ensures that we receive credit for the work you we in standardizing and integrating these two separate complex datasets.

## **Open Science Alignment**
Our project uses public data from Zillow and the U.S. Census Bureau.

The Benefit: Using an open license like MIT (for the code) and Creative Commons (for the data) ensures that our project remains as accessible and "public-good" oriented as the sources it was built upon. There is no distortion.
